Which Thai visa fits your condo lease?

Most Bang Na condo leases run for 12 months. How long your visa lets you stay decides whether you can sign one, or need a shorter lease. Here are the main options in 2026, and what each means for renting.

Last reviewed: October 2026. Rules change often; confirm with the Thai embassy or immigration before you apply.

Visa exemption: now 30 days

From 15 September 2026, visitors from about 60 countries, including the US, UK, Japan, Australia and most of Europe, get 30 days visa-free, down from 60. You can extend once by 30 days at an immigration office for about 1,900 THB. Repeated visa-free entries can lead to questions at the border.

For renting: 30 to 60 days is too short for a standard 12-month lease. Look at monthly rentals, or get a longer visa before you sign.

DTV: for remote workers

The Destination Thailand Visa is valid for 5 years with multiple entries. Each entry gives 180 days, which you can extend once by another 180 days for 1,900 THB. You need 500,000 THB in savings and must work for employers or clients outside Thailand, or join an approved course such as Muay Thai or Thai cooking. You apply online through the Thai e-Visa system, from outside Thailand. The DTV does not allow work for a Thai employer.

For renting: a 6-month lease matches one entry. If you plan to extend, a 12-month lease often costs less per month.

Non-B: working for a Thai company

If a Thai company hires you, it sponsors a Non-Immigrant B visa and a work permit. The stay is usually extended a year at a time while you keep the job.

For renting: a 12-month lease is normal. If your job could move you, ask for an early-termination clause before you sign.

Retirement visa: age 50 and over

You need 800,000 THB in a Thai bank account, or income of 65,000 THB a month, or a mix of the two that adds up to 800,000 THB a year. The money must be in the account two months before you apply. The O-A visa, issued from your home country, also requires health insurance of at least USD 100,000 (or 3,000,000 THB) a year. The Non-O route, extended inside Thailand, does not.

For renting: a 12-month lease lines up with the yearly extension.

LTR and Thailand Privilege: long stays

The LTR visa runs up to 10 years for four groups: wealthy global citizens, wealthy pensioners, people working remotely for large overseas companies, and highly skilled professionals in sectors promoted by the Board of Investment. Holders report their address once a year instead of every 90 days.

Thailand Privilege (formerly Thailand Elite) is a paid membership with a long-stay visa: from 650,000 THB for 5 years (Bronze) to 5,000,000 THB for 20 years (Reserve).

What your landlord needs from you

  • Passport and visa page: a copy goes with the lease.
  • TM30: the owner of the place you stay must report your address to immigration within 24 hours of you moving in. Ask for the receipt. Immigration asks for it when you extend your visa or file a 90-day report.
  • 90-day report: on most long-stay visas you report your address every 90 days of continuous stay. The fine for missing one is 2,000 THB.
